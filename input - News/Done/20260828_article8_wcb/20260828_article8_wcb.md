# Nasdaq Aligns Trading Halt Rules Across Three Affiliated Markets From 10 August

When a stock stops trading, the questions that follow are always the same. Who called the halt? Who decides it is over? And what happens on the other venues where the same shares change hands? Nasdaq has now written all three answers into one coordinated set of rules.

## What the notice does, and when

The exchange set out the changes in **Equity Trader Alert #2026-38: Nasdaq Trading Halt Rules Update**, issued on **Wednesday 8 July 2026** and taking effect on **10 August 2026**.

Three markets are covered: **The Nasdaq Stock Market (Nasdaq), Nasdaq Texas (NTX) and Nasdaq PSX**. The Nasdaq Exchanges are implementing the amendments in coordinated form rather than one venue at a time, and the notice describes their purpose as clarifying three key areas. A trading halt, in plain terms, is a temporary suspension of trading in a security ordered by the exchange.

## First area: who has the authority to halt

The first area is **Regulatory Halt Authority** — the legal footing on which a halt is called. The notice states that this basis includes provisions drawn from **SIP Plans**, along with **discretionary halts** and **mandatory halts**.

That distinction matters because it separates the halts an exchange may choose to impose from those it is required to impose. Setting both out in the same rulebook removes the ambiguity of each market reading its own authority differently.

## Second area: how trading comes back

The second area covers **Trading Resumption Procedures**. Here the amendments spell out how **Primary Listing Markets** — the exchange on which a security is actually listed — initiate a regulatory halt and then resume trading once it ends.

The rules also clarify the obligations of other exchanges during that sequence. In other words, the listing venue keeps the lead role in reopening a stock, and the notice makes explicit what the remaining venues are expected to do while that plays out.

## Third area: halts that stop at one venue

Not every halt needs to travel. The third area, **Operational Halts**, clarifies the discretion of the Nasdaq Exchanges to impose halts **affecting only their own market** — the case where a problem is local to a single venue rather than market-wide.

That discretion is the balance the notice is trying to strike. The amendments promote **"consistent and transparent treatment of events that may affect multiple exchanges"**, according to the alert, while maintaining exchange discretion in certain instances. The operative verb throughout is *clarify* — this is housekeeping on the rulebook, not a tightening or a loosening of when trading stops.

## Filings and system preparation

The alert cites four rule filings behind the changes: **SR-NASDAQ-2022-017_Amendment_1**, **SR-BX-2023-007**, **SR-Phlx-2022-49** and **Nasdaq UTP Amendment 50**.

For firms that need to prepare their systems ahead of the effective date, the notice makes **Saturday UAT production testing** available on **8 August 2026** — two days before the amended rules go live.
