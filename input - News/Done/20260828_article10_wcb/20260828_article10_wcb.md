# Thai SEC Widens Major Shareholder Test for Futures Firms to Cover Board Control and Spouses

The name on the share register is no longer the final answer. Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission (the Thai SEC) has rewritten the major shareholder test for **derivatives business operators** so that it now reaches whoever can appoint or remove half the board — through a stake held directly, or through a chain of holdings.

The revised criteria **took effect on 16 August 2026**. The regulator set out what changed in a release **published on 17 August 2026**.

## What changed, and when it took hold

The Thai SEC says the revision brings the major shareholder criteria for derivatives firms into line with the rules already applied to the **securities business and the digital asset business**. Three licensed activities, in other words, are now measured against a single yardstick.

A **derivatives business operator** is a firm licensed to run a futures or derivatives business — the intermediary that stands between an investor and the market, handling orders in contracts whose value tracks an underlying asset.

## The control test now reaches into the boardroom

The heart of the revision is a broader definition of control. In the regulator's own wording, the major shareholder definition covers **a person with the power to control the appointment or removal of half or more of all directors, whether directly or indirectly**.

Half or more of the board is where a shareholder stops merely holding an interest and starts deciding who runs the company. And **indirectly** matters just as much: an indirect holding is a stake owned through another company or another person rather than in one's own name. The test follows the line of power, not the list of names.

## Family members and coordinated votes are counted in

The criteria also sweep in people close to the shareholder. **Spouses, cohabiting partners and minor children** now count towards the same total, which narrows the oldest gap in any ownership rule — shares parked in a relative's name.

So does **acting in concert**, the situation where several parties who hold their shares separately agree to vote the same way. Under the revised test, the regulator treats that bloc as one.

## The 180-day clock now running for operators

The practical burden falls on the firms. Derivatives business operators must **review their major shareholder status** and file for approval of any shareholder who has never been approved **within 180 days** of the rules taking effect.

An ownership structure that satisfied the earlier criteria may throw up new names once the wider definition is applied.

## The tools behind the rule, and the reason given

Two supporting mechanisms do the arithmetic. Indirect shareholdings are calculated **pro rata** — traced proportionally down each layer of the chain — and the regulator also weighs **significant funding sources**, asking where the money behind a stake came from.

The Thai SEC gives its rationale as improving transparency and reducing money-laundering risk. The clock is running, and the question each operator must answer is no longer who owns it, but who can command its board.
