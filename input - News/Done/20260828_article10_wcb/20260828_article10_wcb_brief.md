## 📊 Key Facts (from raw data — do not add figures)
- **Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission (the Thai SEC)** has revised the **major shareholder criteria for derivatives business operators**, aligning them with the **securities business and digital asset business** regimes
- **Effective 16 August 2026** · the release was **published 17 August 2026**
- **Point 1 — a wider control test:** the definition of a major shareholder now covers a person with the power to control the appointment or removal of **half or more of all directors, whether directly or indirectly**
- **Point 2 — related persons counted in:** **spouses · cohabiting partners · minor children**, and **"acting in concert"** arrangements where several parties coordinate their voting rights
- **Point 3 — the compliance clock:** operators must **review their major shareholder status** and file for approval of shareholders not previously approved **within 180 days** of the rules taking effect
- **Supporting mechanisms:** **pro-rata calculation of indirect shareholdings** and assessment of **significant funding sources** — the SEC gives the rationale as improving transparency and mitigating money-laundering risk

## 🪝 Hook
The name on the share register is no longer the final answer. Thailand's SEC has rewritten the major shareholder test for derivatives firms so that it reaches whoever can appoint or remove half the board — through a direct stake or a chain of them.
