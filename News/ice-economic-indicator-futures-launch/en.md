# ICE Turns Central Bank Decisions Into Futures With Its First Economic Indicator Contracts

The day a central bank sets rates has always moved markets. Now it is in line to get a contract of its own. Intercontinental Exchange, Inc. (NYSE: ICE) announced on 29 June 2026 the planned launch of **its first economic indicator futures contracts**, written on global monetary policy decisions and on US natural gas storage reports.

The announcement carried a London and New York dateline and went out through BUSINESS WIRE. Trading is **planned for 10 August 2026, subject to regulatory completion**.

## What ICE announced

The novelty sits in the underlying, not in the wrapper.

A futures contract is an agreement struck now and settled later, and the familiar versions track the price of something — crude oil, wheat, a stock index. The set ICE has announced tracks the outcome of a policy decision and the reading of a government statistic instead. The company frames the rollout as a planned launch, with its date resting on regulatory work still to be completed.

## What the new contracts track

One family follows **central bank rate decisions** at the U.S. Federal Reserve System, the European Central Bank and the Bank of England, offering exposure to those institutions' scheduled policy meetings. ICE describes the three as "the three most systemically important central banks in the world" — the exchange's own wording, not an independent ranking.

The second family follows **US natural gas storage inventory levels**, which the U.S. Energy Information Administration publishes every week.

## Cash settlement, an exchange venue and a clearing house

Every contract in the set is **cash-settled**: the two sides pay the difference in money, and nothing physical changes hands.

They are also **exchange-traded and centrally-cleared**, meaning trades run through the venue's order book and a clearing house stands between buyer and seller to guarantee settlement. ICE says the design gives market participants a way to express views on specific economic events and decisions.

The release lists ten product codes — OID, OIS, OIR, EUD, EUS, EUR, MPL, MPS, MPR and EWP — without saying which code belongs to which contract.

## The reasoning ICE offers

**Trabue Bland, Senior Vice President of Futures Markets at ICE**, said the move "reflects demand for regulated onshore products that allow customers to take positions on economically relevant risks that shape markets."

He added that the products leverage the global trading and clearing platform the company has built, calling them a "new approach to hedging significant moments impacting global markets." The description of the contracts as innovative, and the hedging claim itself, belong to the issuer.

## Where the launch sits in ICE's line-up

The contracts follow the recent launch of **ICE's Polymarket Signals and Sentiment** service, an exclusive prediction data and analytics offering. It supplies normalized data feeds representing Polymarket's prediction markets, letting professional and institutional traders read crowd-sourced probability assessments as market signals.

ICE itself is a Fortune 500 company whose futures, equity and options exchanges include the New York Stock Exchange, and which also runs clearing houses.

Should the regulatory process reach its end, a rate decision will no longer only move markets — it will carry a price of its own.
