# KKPS Takes 24.33% of Thai Equity Turnover on 18 August, Nearly Triple the Runner-Up

In a single session, one broker handled close to a quarter of all the order flow on the Thai market. Broker market-share data for **18 August 2569** puts **Kiatnakin Phatra Securities (KKPS)** at the head of the table with **24.33%**, nearly three times the **9.19%** booked by **KGI** in second place.

## The session at a glance

Total securities turnover on the Thai market for the 18 August 2569 session came to **83,308.95 million baht**. Measured against that pool of trading, the ten brokers that carried the most business on the day line up like this.

| Rank | Broker | Market share (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | KKPS | 24.33 |
| 2 | KGI | 9.19 |
| 3 | JPM | 8.25 |
| 4 | UBS | 5.91 |
| 5 | MST | 5.21 |
| 6 | CGSI | 4.58 |
| 7 | BLS | 4.04 |
| 8 | KINGSFORD | 3.56 |
| 9 | FSS | 3.34 |
| 10 | INVX | 2.82 |

## The gap between the leader and the chasing pack

What stands out in the table is not the order of the names but the distance between them. KKPS holds **24.33%** of the session's order flow. Everyone else is in single digits.

**KGI** takes second place at **9.19%**, followed by **JPM** at **8.25%** and **UBS** at **5.91%**. Those three sit close enough together to read as one group, while the top of the table stands on a level of its own — a shape that points to order flow concentrated in one house on the day.

## A tight band from fifth to tenth

Further down the list, the spaces between the names narrow again. **MST** holds **5.21%**, ahead of **CGSI** at **4.58%** and **BLS** at **4.04%**. **KINGSFORD** follows at **3.56%**, then **FSS** at **3.34%**, with **INVX** closing the table at **2.82%**.

All six of those brokers fall inside a band running from **2.82% to 5.21%**, so the step from one rank to the next is a fraction of a percentage point — a very different picture from the space at the top.

## How to read the table

The ranking measures one thing: the combined value of the buy and sell orders investors routed through each broker. It is not a measure of a firm's earnings, its client base or the size of its balance sheet. A broker's position simply shows how much of the day's trading passed through its systems.

The table is also a snapshot of a **single trading session**, not a cumulative monthly or annual league table. The figures come from **Aspen and the Stock Exchange of Thailand**, in a report published on **19 August 2569 at 08:01**.
